Undergraduate Research

Harvard University — Capasso Lab

Research internship · May 2023 – January 2024 · Department of Applied Physics · Federico Capasso's research group

I joined the Capasso group as a visiting undergraduate research intern for eight months, working on metasurfaces — flat optical elements patterned with subwavelength nanostructures that allow you to engineer wavefronts. My work spanned two main projects and the full pipeline behind them: design, simulation, fabrication files, and experiment.

Multiplexed metasurface holography

I contributed to designing a metasurface capable of producing multiple distinct dichroic holographic images from a single device. Exploring how much independent information can be encoded into, and reconstructed from, one flat optic through careful wavefront engineering.

Metasurfaces · holography · wavefront engineering

Electrically tunable metasurfaces

The second project integrated nonlinear electro-optic materials with metasurface guided-mode resonances to make metasurfaces whose optical response can be actively tuned with an applied electric field — a step toward reconfigurable flat optics.

Nonlinear optics · guided-mode resonance · active tuning

How I worked

I arrived with limited hands-on optics experience and learned fast. On the computational side, I helped develop custom FDTD (finite-difference time-domain) simulations in Lumerical, evaluating over 90,000 nanostructures to build a comprehensive design library, then used MATLAB filtering algorithms to select the best structures and generate the GDS files used for cleanroom fabrication. On the experimental side, I built and aligned a broadband free-space optical setup spanning the visible to the infrared, and automated the entire characterization process in Python to greatly increase measurement throughput.

This work contributed to two manuscripts in preparation, on which I'm a co-author.

University of Waterloo — Strickland Lab

Research internship · January – May 2024 · Department of Physics and Astronomy · co-supervised by Nobel laureate Dr. Donna Strickland

Back at Waterloo, I continued the thread of nonlinear optical materials I had picked up at Harvard, now in the Strickland Lab. The focus shifted toward modelling and characterizing how light behaves in multilayer optical structures.

What I worked on

  • Optical data analysis of experimental measurements
  • Nonlinear optical materials and their wavelength-dependent behavior
  • Multilayer thin-film modelling using transfer-matrix methods
  • Comparing simulated spectral responses against experimental characterization

This built directly on the optical-materials work from Harvard, deepening my experience with simulation and data analysis for real optical devices.
