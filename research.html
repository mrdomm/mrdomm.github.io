Graduate research

My M.Sc. in Physics at the Institute for Quantum Computing, in the Quantum Photonic Devices Lab, centers on building scalable quantum photonic systems — from the devices that generate single and entangled photons to the platforms that route and control them.

This page collects what I'm working on: my active research projects, thesis focus, committee, publications, and the techniques I use day to day.

Research projects

My main lines of work during the master's. Each is at a different stage — some are ongoing, others heading toward publication.

Ongoing

Quantum dot entangled photon sources

Developing on-demand sources of polarization-entangled photon pairs from semiconductor quantum dots for quantum communication and networking.

Role: Lead researcher Techniques: Cryogenic optics, single-photon detection, correlation measurement
Ongoing

Integrated photonics platform

Designing and characterizing on-chip components to route, filter, and manipulate photons from quantum-dot sources.

Role: Researcher Techniques: Cleanroom nanofabrication, FDTD simulation
Exploratory

Metasurface-based optical control

Exploring flat-optic elements for shaping and steering photonic states, building on prior metasurface work at Harvard.

Role: Researcher Techniques: Transfer matrix methods, nanofabrication

Thesis focus

My thesis investigates how to build bright, high-fidelity sources of entangled photons that can plug into scalable, chip-based photonic systems — bridging the gap between excellent single-device performance and the integration needed for real quantum networks.

The work combines device design and simulation, cleanroom fabrication, and experimental characterization, with the goal of sources that are both high-quality and manufacturable.
