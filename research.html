Research

I'm an experimental physicist working at the intersection of light and nanoscale devices. My research spans quantum photonics, integrated photonics, nanophotonics, metasurfaces, nonlinear optics, experimental optical systems, and nanofabrication.

Over time my focus has evolved from shaping light with nanostructures toward building the devices that generate and control quantum light. Below is a quick tour — dive into the detailed pages for the full story.

Current · 2025 – present

Master's Research

M.Sc. Physics · University of Waterloo · Institute for Quantum Computing · Quantum Photonic Devices Lab

I'm developing on-demand, quantum-dot sources of entangled photons and working toward integrating quantum photonic devices into scalable photonic platforms. The aim is bright, high-quality entangled-photon sources suitable for quantum communication and future quantum networks. Supervisor: Prof. Michael E. Reimer.

Prior · 2023 – 2024

Undergraduate Research

Harvard University, Capasso Lab · University of Waterloo, Strickland Lab

At Harvard I worked on metasurfaces and wavefront engineering — including multiplexed holography and tunable metasurfaces — using large-scale FDTD simulations and automated optical characterization. At Waterloo I continued into nonlinear optical materials with optical data analysis and transfer-matrix modelling.
